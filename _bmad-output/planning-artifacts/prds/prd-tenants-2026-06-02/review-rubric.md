# PRD Quality Review — Tenants Management UI

## Overall verdict

This is a strong, unusually disciplined PRD. Its thesis — that in an eventually-consistent, event-sourced system the UI's job is to *refuse to fake certainty* — is stated plainly (§1, §6) and genuinely drives the feature set, the metrics, the counter-metrics, and the phasing. The §6 interaction contract (CP-1..CP-10) is the spine of the document and is referenced by ID rather than re-explained, which makes downstream extraction clean. What's at risk is concentrated and honest: a meaningful slice of committed product (FR-22/24/25) has no backlog or backend backing, every command flow is gated on FrontComposer components that don't exist with *no fallback approved yet*, and a few done-ness gaps (the "Users" primary area has no FR; some preview/limit thresholds defer to "domain limits" without a visible bound). None of these are smoothed over — they are flagged inline — so the PRD is decision-ready about its own risk, which is the right failure mode for a chain-top spec.

## Decision-readiness — strong

A decision-maker can act on this. The hardest trade-offs are named *as* trade-offs with what was given up, not buried as "considerations." The clearest example is the optimistic-vs-confirmed-only choice: CP-3/CP-4 mandate that "the UI **never shows success it has not confirmed against the source-of-truth projection**," and addendum §F states this was "chosen deliberately over optimistic success, **against the more common SaaS pattern**, because correctness under eventual consistency is the product's core trust proposition." That is a real bet with its cost (slower-feeling UI) acknowledged. CP-6 makes another genuinely contested call — the last-owner and last-global-administrator cases are "warnings with extra friction, **never** backend-prohibited … the system supports deliberately reducing a tenant to zero owners" — which is the kind of decision a reviewer could push on, and it is stated rather than hedged.

The Open Questions (§16) are mostly *actually open* — Q3 (layout contract gating the MVP), Q6 (RTL "in or out for v1?"), Q9 (hide vs. stub the Audit area), Q10 (freshness thresholds) have no answer smuggled into the next clause. The two `[NOTE FOR PM]` callouts sit at real tensions (§7.4 FR-12 blocked on three missing components; §7.9 FR-22/24/25 unbacked), not at safe checkpoints.

### Findings
- **low** Open-question phrasing occasionally leans rhetorical (§16) — Q12 (ID-scheme correction) and Q2 (fallback approval) read as decisions already made by the PRD ("The specs should be corrected"; "secure Product/UX approval"), i.e. directives rather than open questions. *Fix:* move settled directives to Risks/Dependencies and keep §16 for genuinely unresolved choices, so the open-items count reflects true openness.

## Substance over theater — strong

The content is earned, not furniture. Personas are restrained: three role groups (operator, owner, member) with named exemplars (Elena, Sofia, Nadia, Marc) where each name carries a *distinct* job — Elena triages and acts (UJ-1/2/3/6), Sofia investigates and recovers (UJ-4), Nadia self-serves (UJ-5), Marc only self-audits. None is decorative; each maps to FRs and phases. This clears the "more than four personas / personas that drive no decision" bar comfortably.

The distinctiveness claim is not innovation theater — §1's "honesty about state" is the same thing the §6 contract enforces and the SM-C counter-metrics protect, so the novelty is structural rather than asserted. The NFRs avoid boilerplate: NFR-3 doesn't say "reliable," it says the UI "treats the projection as source of truth, re-queries to confirm, and is correct under **at-least-once delivery and projection lag**" — a product-specific condition. The Vision (§1) could not swap into another PRD in this category: it is specific to event-sourced compensating-command correction and "never editing history or the data store."

The one place to watch: §5.2's tone paragraph ("professional, calm, precise operations console — not marketing") is closer to mood-board prose, but it is short and earns its place by setting the no-color-only and density constraints that §5 then makes testable.

## Strategic coherence — strong

There is a thesis and the document bets on it: *trust under eventual consistency*. Feature prioritization follows from it rather than from ease — the MVP is deliberately the **read-only foundation** ("see the truth" before "act safely"), and §14.2 is candid that the deferred command flows are not the easy ones being saved for later but are blocked by missing infrastructure ("There are no command flows that are unblocked today"). That is thesis-driven sequencing, not "what's easy first."

The Success Metrics validate the thesis rather than measuring raw activity: SM-2 (time-to-answer "who has access / what changed") measures the core "see the truth" value, and the counter-metrics are the strongest signal of coherence in the document — SM-C1 ("destructive actions completed **without** viewing a Consequence Preview should stay at ~0 … We do **not** want to 'reduce friction' by removing the preview"), SM-C2 (a *drop* in surfaced errors is a red flag, not a win), SM-C3 (any rise in acting on stale data is a regression "even if it speeds tasks"). These explicitly defend the thesis against being optimized away. The MVP scope kind is coherent — a problem-solving/experience foundation, with platform-altitude command work explicitly phased behind it.

### Findings
- **low** SM-3 (self-service uptake) has no paired counter-metric (§15) — the three SM-C metrics counterbalance SM-1/SM-2/SM-4 but not the self-service push, which could incentivize nudging owners into changes they don't fully understand. *Fix:* consider a counter-metric guarding owner-initiated changes that skip the consequence preview, or note explicitly why SM-C1 already covers it.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate

This is the dimension carrying the most weight downstream, and it is mostly good but not uniformly unforgiving. The strength: most FRs carry an explicit, testable **Consequences** block, and several are model-grade. FR-1 enumerates the exact state set that must not collapse ("loading, empty, filtered-empty, error, stale, degraded") and bounds behavior ("sorting or paging **never** hides a pending or stale marker"). FR-12 is exhaustive — required inputs validated before preview, NoOp/duplicate handling, "unable to verify" never shown as success, every failure mode mapped to a recovery. The §9 acceptance-evidence section converts "accessible" into named required scenarios (stale projection, rejected command, unknown confirmation, audit unavailable, last-owner warning, permission-missing) and concrete responsive widths, which is far above adjective-level. The §6 contract gives every command FR an inherited, ID-referenced acceptance spine.

The gaps are specific:
- Performance is bounded *only* for audit (~500 events; §7.8 NFR, NFR-1). Read surfaces (tenant list, member table) carry no latency or page-size bound — NFR-1 says reads "are cheap" via conditional requests, which is a mechanism, not a threshold.
- Several FR consequences defer the testable bound to "domain limits" without surfacing it: FR-16 ("values exceeding **domain limits** are rejected") and FR-11/FR-14 (validation errors "surface as safe localized field messages") leave the *trigger* condition unstated at product altitude. Defensible since the backend owns the rule, but an engineer can't write the boundary test from the PRD alone.

### Findings
- **medium** Read-surface performance is unbounded (§8 NFR-1; §7.1 FR-1) — only audit has a numeric target (~500 events). The tenant list and member table, the MVP's entire reason for existing, have no page-size, render, or latency bound. *Fix:* add a target page size / time-to-interactive bound for the list and member-table reads, or state explicitly that cursor + conditional-request behavior is the only contract and why no latency target applies.
- **medium** "Users" primary navigation area has no scoped FR (§5.1, §14.1) — §14.1 lists "Users … areas **functioning** (read)" as in-scope MVP, but the only user-facing reads (FR-3 My-Tenants, FR-4 user-lookup) sit under §7.1 Tenant Discovery and addendum §A maps them to ui-01/ui-02, not a Users surface. An engineer cannot tell what "Users area functioning" means. *Fix:* either bind FR-4 (and FR-3) explicitly to the Users area with its own done-state, or demote "Users" from the MVP in-scope list to match the FR coverage.
- **low** Boundary triggers deferred to "domain limits" (§7.6 FR-16; §7.5 FR-14; §7.4 FR-11) — the rejection *behavior* is testable but the *threshold* that triggers it is not visible. *Fix:* reference the canonical domain-limit source in the addendum so story authors can locate the boundary, even if the PRD doesn't restate the numbers.

## Scope honesty — strong

Omissions are explicit and do real work. §13 Non-Goals is substantive and reasoned, not a disclaimer list: email/invitation flows are out *because* "the backend has no invitation concept," data-store edits are out *because* corrections are compensating commands only, mobile high-impact flows are out, building FrontComposer components inside Tenants is out (with the boundary rationale in §11). The most important act of scope honesty is §7.9's `[NOTE FOR PM]`: FR-22/24/25 "are committed product intent but are **not yet backed by a dedicated `ui-NN` backlog row or backend evidence** … need a future story before they are build-ready" — and addendum §A repeats it in the mapping table ("**no backlog row yet**"). De-scoping risk is surfaced, not done silently.

Open-items density is appropriate for the stakes. The PRD is *not* a green-light-to-build for the whole vision — it explicitly green-lights only the read-only MVP and documents the rest as phased and gated. Against that framing, ~12 Open Questions + ~10 indexed assumptions + 2 PM notes is proportionate, not a blocker, because the open items cluster on the deferred phases (commands, audit, recovery) rather than on the MVP being shipped. The one cross-cutting caveat — "**No fallback is approved yet**" (§4, §11, R-4) — is stated repeatedly and correctly treated as a gate.

### Findings
- **medium** No `[NON-GOAL for MVP]` callout distinguishes "deferred phase" from "never" within scope sections (§7) — features like FR-12/FR-19 are phased (2c) and features like compensating recovery are conditionally committed, but a reader skimming §7 sees them as committed FRs; only §14 and the §7.9 note carry the deferral. *Fix:* tag phase-deferred and not-yet-backed FRs inline at their definition (the phase label helps, but an explicit not-in-MVP marker would prevent silent assumption that all 25 FRs are the build target).

## Downstream usability — strong

This PRD is built to be source-extracted, which matters because it is explicitly chain-top (§0: "feeds UX/architecture/epic owners"). The Glossary (§4) is thorough and every domain noun (Truth State Badge, Freshness, Consequence Preview, Compensating command, Support-safe reference) is used verbatim across FRs, UJs, and SMs. IDs are contiguous and unique — FR-1..25, UJ-1..6, CP-1..10, NFR-1..5, R-1..6, SM-1..5 + SM-C1..C3 — with no gaps or duplicates, and cross-references resolve (FRs cite CP-N and §H/§G; UJs cite later UJs and §13/§16). The canonical-state-set discipline (CP-10, addendum §G) — defining enumerations once in the truth-state spec and *mirroring* rather than restating them — is exactly what prevents drift when UX and architecture pull from the same vocabulary.

Each section stands alone: §6 is referenced by ID from §7 rather than "see above," and the addendum is a clean navigational bridge (feature → backlog → spec → FrontComposer dependency) rather than a re-spec. UJs each have a named protagonist carrying context inline (Elena, Sofia, Nadia) — no floating UJs. The addendum §A explicitly reconciles the FR/backlog cardinality mismatch ("ui-09 is one backlog row covering both add-user and change-role … the PRD splits it into FR-10/FR-11"), which is precisely the kind of note epic creation needs.

### Findings
- **low** SM→FR cross-references have minor coverage asymmetry (§15) — SM-2 validates "FR-1..FR-9" but FR-5/FR-6/FR-7 (overview, config view, copy) are weakly related to "time-to-answer who-has-access"; SM-4 lists FR-13/FR-10/FR-16 but not FR-14. Harmless for reading, but story-traceability tooling that round-trips SM↔FR will show noise. *Fix:* tighten the validated-FR lists to the FRs each metric genuinely measures.

## Shape fit — strong

The shape matches the product. This is the rubric's "multi-stakeholder B2B / meaningful UX" case crossed with the "internal+self-service operations UI" framing, and the PRD treats UJs as load-bearing (six named-protagonist journeys with entry state, path, climax, resolution, and edge cases) — correct, because two distinct audiences (operators vs. owners) traverse the same surfaces under different authorization, and the journeys are where that divergence is made concrete (UJ-5's edge case: Nadia "never sees other tenants or the Global Administrators surface"). It is not over-formalized: there is no UJ-per-FR padding, and the read-only MVP's operational metrics (SM-2 time-to-answer) sit alongside user-facing ones, matching the operations-console nature.

The §5 visual/IA and §9 accessibility depth are proportionate to a launch/enterprise-stakes admin tool, not gold-plating — the safety-critical-column rule and fail-closed responsive rule (§5.3) are product-specific, not generic responsive boilerplate. Brownfield handling is correct: the PRD sits above existing Epic 9 specs (§0), references them as source of technical depth, and R-6/§16.12/addendum §E explicitly flags where the *existing specs are wrong* (the ULID ID-scheme error) rather than inheriting the error — good brownfield hygiene.

## Mechanical notes

- **ID continuity:** Clean. FR-1..25, UJ-1..6, CP-1..10, NFR-1..5, R-1..6, SM-1..5 + SM-C1..C3 all contiguous and unique. No gaps, no duplicates.
- **Cross-references resolve:** Spot-checked CP-N citations in §7, §H/§G references, and §16/§13 back-references — all resolve. The §0 doc-list and addendum spec filenames are consistent.
- **Assumptions Index roundtrip:** Clean. Ten inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags (plus two embedded in narrative prose at §3.3/§7.6) all map to the ten grouped entries in §17; every §17 entry traces back to an inline location. The §0 meta-mention of the tagging convention is not itself an assumption (correctly not indexed).
- **Glossary drift:** None material. "Truth State Badge," "Consequence Preview," "Command Lifecycle Panel," "Freshness," "Compensating command" used with consistent casing throughout. Lifecycle-state tokens appear in two notations (`projection-confirmed` hyphenated in the PRD body §6/§7 vs. `projection_confirmed` underscored in addendum §G) — cosmetic, but worth normalizing so downstream string-matching doesn't treat them as distinct.
- **UJ protagonist naming:** Every UJ (1–6) names its protagonist (Elena/Sofia/Nadia) and carries entry-state context inline. No floating UJs.
- **Required sections for stakes/type:** All present — Vision, Why Now, Users + JTBD + Non-Users + UJs, Glossary, IA/Visual, cross-cutting contract, Features/FRs, NFRs, A11y/L10n, Guardrails, Dependencies, Risks, Non-Goals, MVP/Phasing, Success Metrics + counters, Open Questions, Assumptions Index, plus a technical addendum. Complete for a chain-top launch-stakes PRD.
