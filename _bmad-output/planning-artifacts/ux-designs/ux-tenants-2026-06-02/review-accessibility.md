# Accessibility Review — Tenants Management UI (DESIGN.md + EXPERIENCE.md)

> Reviewer: skeptical a11y review against WCAG 2.1 AA (and 2.2 AA where claimed) + the PRD accessibility floor (digest §7). Scope: the two UX spines at `ux-tenants-2026-06-02/`, cross-checked against `.decision-log.md` and the verified Fluent UI Blazor v5 icon set (icon names confirmed present via the Fluent MCP against pkg `5.0.0.26139`).

## Verdict

These spines are unusually disciplined on accessibility intent — the no-color-only rule, the absolute-timestamp mandate, the inline (not hover) disabled reasons, the complete-OR-exit guarantee, the focus-return rule, and the reserved-`Assertive` policy are all stated, and they are *load-bearing* on the product's honesty thesis rather than bolted on. But the spines also lean their entire forced-colors safety story on "icon + text still carries the meaning when the OS overrides the fill," and **the chosen icon set does not actually hold that promise**: at least two glyphs (`Prohibited`, `Clock`) are each reused across two *different semantic roles*, so in forced-colors — exactly the mode where the spine says color drops out and the icon must carry the load — those states collapse to identical icon + differ only by text, which is precisely the single-channel dependency the design claims to forbid. Separately, the `Assertive`-vs-`Polite` rule is specified as a *politeness reservation* but never reconciled with the *color-role* binding it shares a vocabulary with: `Danger` (the assertive trigger) also covers routine `risk high` badges and the resting destructive-control, so a naive role→live-region mapping would over-announce, and the genuinely-assertive states (`unable to verify`, destructive-block, `degraded`) are *not* `Danger`-colored and would be *missed* by a role-driven mapping. Both are correctable in the spine without changing the architecture, but until the icon set is de-duplicated, the forced-colors evidence row in the ready-gate cannot honestly be cited as met, and several "we handle this" claims are deferred to FrontComposer (`FC-A11Y`, `FC-DOC`) or to "verify at build" without a tracked owner. **Net: strong floor, two real Blocker-class gaps in the no-color-only + live-region guarantees, plus a cluster of High items around contrast verification ownership and the keyboard/SR obligations that the spine asserts but does not yet bind to acceptance evidence.**

## Findings

| ID | Location | Severity | Issue | Suggested fix |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A11Y-01 | DESIGN.md §truth-state-badge icon table (lines 195-217): `Prohibited` mapped to **both** `rejected` (Danger) and tenant `disabled` (Severe) | **Blocker** | The spine's forced-colors guarantee is "the badge fill may be overridden by the OS and the **icon + text must still carry the meaning**" (line 144). But `rejected` and `disabled` then render as the *same glyph* (`Prohibited`) and differ only by hue (gone in forced-colors) + text label. That is a single-channel (text-only) dependency in the exact mode the rule exists for, and it conflates a *refusal/failure* (Danger, tier 3) with a *legitimate blocker* (Severe, tier 2) — the very Severe-vs-Danger distinction the color section calls out as load-bearing. A color-blind user in normal mode is in the same position. | Give `disabled` a distinct non-`Prohibited` glyph (e.g. a power/plug or lock-style mark) so Severe-tier "blocked, not broken" is iconographically separate from Danger-tier `rejected`. Re-verify the chosen name in the pinned package. Add an acceptance check: every state pair that shares a `BadgeColor` family must have *distinct* glyphs, and every state pair that shares a *glyph* must have distinct color **and** distinct text — never glyph-collision + color-collision. |
| A11Y-02 | DESIGN.md icon table: `Clock` mapped to **both** Informative in-flight (`submitted`/`accepted`/`pending`/`projection_pending`, lines 198) **and** Warning `aging` (line 202) | **Blocker** | Same defect as A11Y-01 on the most safety-relevant axis. In forced-colors, an in-flight `accepted` (Informative — "wait, not done") and an `aging` freshness (Warning — "usable with friction") become the *same* `Clock` glyph + a text label. The spine's own firewall narrative ("in-flight is not done", line 198) depends on `accepted` reading as distinctly *not* success and *not* a freshness-caution; collapsing them to one glyph weakens that exactly when color is unavailable. Note `aging` and the in-flight cluster also sit adjacent in tables, so the confusion is realistic, not theoretical. | Pick a distinct in-flight glyph that is not a clock face (the firewall already forbids a checkmark; an `ArrowSync`/`Hourglass`/`ClockArrowDownload`-type "in progress" mark would separate "work in flight" from "data aging"). Keep `Clock` for the *freshness* family only, or vice-versa — but the two semantic families must not share a glyph. Re-verify the name. |
| A11Y-03 | EXPERIENCE.md §Accessibility Floor (line 148) + §truth-state-badge (line 77) + DESIGN.md destructive-control (line 287) & badge color rule | **Blocker** | The live-region rule is stated as a *politeness reservation* (`Assertive` for rejection/failure/unable-to-verify/destructive-block; `Polite` else) but is never reconciled with the **color-role** binding it shares tokens with. Two failure directions: (a) **over-announce** — `Danger` is also the color of a routine `risk high` badge (line 204) and the *resting* destructive-control (line 287); if the live-region politeness is derived from `BadgeColor==Danger`, merely rendering a high-risk row or a remove button would fire an assertive announcement, deafening the channel the PRD reserves. (b) **under-announce** — the genuinely assertive states `unable to verify` and `degraded` are **Important** and **Severe** respectively (lines 211-213, badge table), *not* `Danger`; and "destructive-block" (e.g. last-global-admin surfaced as unavailable) renders via `unavailable-action-reason` colored `Important`/`Severe`, not `Danger`. A role-driven mapping would *miss* all of these — the spine's stated assertive triggers would be announced `Polite` or not at all. | State explicitly that live-region politeness is bound to a **dedicated announcement-intent field on the event**, NOT to `BadgeColor` or `MessageBarIntent`. Enumerate the exact assertive set as discrete tokens: `rejected`, `failed`, `unable to verify`, `degraded`, and the destructive-block surfacing — independent of their badge color. Add a Don't: "never derive `AriaLive` from `BadgeColor`/`MessageBarIntent`." Add an acceptance scenario asserting that a `risk high` badge and a resting destructive-control produce **no** assertive announcement. |
| A11Y-04 | DESIGN.md §truth-state-badge appearance rule (lines 144, 188, 299) — `BadgeAppearance.Filled` reserved for Danger + Severe as the "extra non-chromatic weight" | **High** | The spine sells `Filled` as the non-color differentiator that gives the two action-stopping tiers weight "a heavier fill, not merely a different hue" (line 144). In **forced-colors / Windows High Contrast**, the OS overrides background fills with system colors, so `Tint` vs `Filled` frequently collapses to the same system canvas/border treatment — the "heavier fill" can disappear precisely in the mode cited. So `Filled` is not a reliable non-chromatic signal there; the only forced-colors-safe differentiators are **glyph shape** and **text** (which A11Y-01/02 show are themselves colliding). | Do not count `Filled` toward the no-color-only guarantee in forced-colors. Require a forced-colors acceptance shot proving Danger/Severe remain distinguishable from Tint roles by **glyph + text alone** (which forces A11Y-01/02 to be fixed first). Consider an explicit non-fill marker for the two stop tiers (e.g. a leading rule/border via `forced-color-adjust` opt-in, or a distinct glyph weight) verified to survive forced-colors. |
| A11Y-05 | DESIGN.md frontmatter "verify against pinned package at build" (lines 11-12, 44, 115, 176, 191, 301) + EXPERIENCE.md Accessibility Floor (no contrast values) | **High** | Every contrast guarantee is delegated to "Fluent owns the rendered token value; verify at build," but **no owner, no gate, and no acceptance row** is attached to that verification. WCAG 1.4.3 (text 4.5:1) and 1.4.11 (non-text/UI 3:1) are *not* automatically satisfied by inheriting Fluent semantic roles — `BadgeAppearance.Tint` renders low-saturation text-on-tinted-fill, and `Subtle`/`Informative` tints in particular are common contrast-failure points; the small (16/20) status glyphs must independently clear 3:1 against the tinted fill. The spine asserts a11y but defers the actual measurement with no tracked owner. | Add a tracked obligation (owner = UX or the FrontComposer/`FC-TOK` team) to measure, per theme (light/dark/HC) and per `BadgeColor`×`BadgeAppearance` pair actually used, both 4.5:1 (label text) and 3:1 (glyph + any border) — and to record results in the ready-gate evidence set. Flag `Tint` + `Subtle`/`Informative` as the highest-risk combos to check first. Make this a Blocker for any *flow* before that flow's stories go `ready`. |
| A11Y-06 | DESIGN.md §unavailable-action-reason (lines 236-241) & member-table action cell (line 265); EXPERIENCE.md (lines 79, 84, 151) | **High** | The inline-reason rule is correctly stated, but the *member-table* exposes these reasons inside a `FluentDataGrid` action cell while the surface is "read-only; must not imply mutation." Two gaps: (1) no spec for how the disabled-action **reason is programmatically associated** with the control it replaces — for a screen-reader user tabbing the grid, an inline reason that is a sibling text node (not `aria-describedby` on the would-be control, or not in the cell's accessible name) is "inline-visible" to sighted users but invisible in the SR reading order. (2) When the action slot shows a *reason instead of a control*, there may be **no focusable element**, so a keyboard/SR user scanning by interactive element never lands on it. | Require that each unavailable-action reason is either (a) the accessible name/description of a present-but-disabled control (`aria-disabled` + `aria-describedby`), or (b) a row-level status text included in the row's accessible relationships — and that it is reachable in the SR reading order, not only visually inline. Add an NVDA acceptance step: navigate a row whose action is unavailable and confirm the reason is announced. |
| A11Y-07 | EXPERIENCE.md §Interaction Primitives "complete-OR-exit" (line 146) + §Accessibility Floor (lines 146-147); consequence-preview & destructive modal (lines 81, 86, 133) | **High** | The complete-OR-exit guarantee, focus-trap, safe-non-committing escape, and focus-return are all *asserted as obligations* but **bound to no acceptance scenario and no owner**, and the underlying primitive is `FC-A11Y` (`needs-confirmation`) which the spine itself says is "non-removable even under fallback" — i.e. the guarantee currently rests on an unconfirmed capability. There is also no statement of what happens to focus/trap if the modal's content **fails closed** (the consequence-preview blocks submission when an item is missing): does focus stay trapped with a reachable exit, or can a user be trapped in a non-submittable dialog? | Bind each clause to the existing required acceptance scenarios (the spine already lists "stale projection, rejected command, unknown confirmation, audit unavailable, last-owner warning, permission-missing") by adding the keyboard/focus assertions to each. Add an explicit scenario: "consequence-preview opens, an item is unavailable, submission is blocked — keyboard user can still exit via Esc/cancel and focus returns to the launching row." Name the owner of the `FC-A11Y` confirmation and make it a ready-gate blocker. |
| A11Y-08 | DESIGN.md icon table risk `high` → `Warning` glyph (line 204) while role is **Danger**; and `degraded` → `ShieldError` (Severe, line 208) | **High** | Two icon/role honesty mismatches that hurt color-blind disambiguation: (1) risk `high` uses the **`Warning`** triangle glyph but is colored **Danger** (tier 3). The triangle is the universal *caution* (tier-1 Warning) symbol; pairing the caution glyph with the refusal color sends contradictory non-color signals and, in forced-colors, makes a *high-risk* state read iconographically as a *mild* caution. (2) `degraded` (a success-prohibited state per CP-3) uses `ShieldError`, which is easily confused with the authorization shields (`ShieldProhibited`, `ShieldQuestion`) used for *permission* states — a different semantic axis. | For risk `high`, choose a glyph that reads as high-severity and is not the tier-1 caution triangle, or accept the triangle only if Warning-tier `aging`/`timeout`/`audit delayed` do **not** also use triangle glyphs (they currently use clock-family glyphs, so a triangle for Danger-risk is *especially* misleading). | A11Y-09 | DESIGN.md icon table `eligible`/`already applied`/`duplicate`/risk `low` → `CheckmarkCircleHint` (Subtle, line 214) vs `confirmed`/`current`/`active` → `CheckmarkCircle` (Success, line 195) | **Medium** | `CheckmarkCircleHint` and `CheckmarkCircle` are deliberately near-identical glyphs (Hint is the faint-outline variant). The firewall's whole point is that Success (proven) must never be confused with not-proven/benign states. In forced-colors the Success green is gone, and a faint-vs-solid checkmark distinction is exactly the kind of subtle differentiator HC/low-vision users lose. So `already applied` (benign, Subtle) could read as `confirmed` (proven, Success) on icon shape alone. | Use a clearly *non-checkmark* glyph for the Subtle benign family (e.g. an info/dot or a "no-change" mark) so "nothing to do here" is never one anti-aliased pixel away from "proven done." | A11Y-10 | EXPERIENCE.md §State Patterns casing rule (lines 90-96) + §truth-state-badge accessible name (line 77) | **Medium** | Canonical tokens are casing-significant and deliberately *not* unified (`audit pending` vs `audit_pending`), and the badge's accessible name is "role + state." | A11Y-11 | EXPERIENCE.md §State Patterns / Truth State Badge 13-state vs DESIGN.md icon table | **Medium** | The badge's canonical 13-state set (line 92) includes `blocked`, but the DESIGN.md icon table has no row for a bare `blocked` state (it maps authorization `blocked` to `ShieldProhibited`, line 212, and `eligible` to a checkmark-hint). ## Strengths (do NOT change these)

- **No-color-only is correctly elevated to a first-class, absolute rule** with the right mechanism (`BadgeColor` + `IconStart` + `IconLabel`/text in one `FluentBadge`), and the rule is explicitly extended to light/dark/high-contrast/forced-colors. The *intent* is exactly right; only the icon-set execution (A11Y-01/02/08/09) needs fixing.
- **The Success-is-proven firewall enforced at the color layer** is excellent and genuinely accessibility-relevant: refusing to let the UI "turn green" until projection-confirmed prevents a whole class of false-reassurance failures for *all* users, and the explicit `accepted`→Informative trap-callout is the right call.
- **Absolute, culture-formatted, monospace timestamps** (no relative-only) directly satisfy the PRD floor and the mono role for glyph-similar ids is a real low-vision/SR win — keep `{typography.mono}` for ids + times.
- **Disabled-action reasons are inline-visible by rule, never hover/tooltip-only**, with the "tooltip may supplement but never be the only explanation" caveat stated. (A11Y-06 is about *programmatic association*, not the inline principle, which is correct.)
- **Reserved-`Assertive` policy exists and is correctly scoped to rejection/failure/unable-to-verify/destructive-block** with `Polite` as default — the *policy* is right; A11Y-03/13 are about how it's *bound*, not the reservation itself.
- **Complete-OR-exit, focus-trap with safe non-committing escape, and focus-return to the launching control** are all explicitly stated as standalone obligations (not assumed from focus order) — this is the correct framing; it just needs acceptance binding (A11Y-07).
- **Table semantics (headers/sort/row relationships) required on all three grids**, with safety-critical columns pinned rather than dropped, and "sorting/paging must never hide a pending or stale marker" — strong, and the read-only member-table's "must not imply mutation" is the right accessibility-of-affordance instinct.
- **Stable layout / reserved footprints (no reflow on badge/action/reason change)** is an underrated a11y win — it prevents focus loss and SR-virtual-buffer churn when state updates. Keep it.
- **Canonical vocabulary used verbatim, casing-significant, never collapsed** — distinct states staying distinct is good for SR users too (they hear the true state); the only addition needed is firewalling the *machine token* out of the *accessible name* (A11Y-10).
- **Icon names were actually verified against the pinned package** (confirmed independently here — all names resolve), which is more rigor than most spines show; the remaining gap is *semantic distinctness*, not existence.
